# Timeline

Every event of the Amazon ML Challenge 2026 project, in time order (IST), from the first message on Fri 25 Sep 2026 at 11:04 to Sun 4 Oct 2026, with its sources.
The Grand Finale is on Wed 7 Oct. The source extracts overlap; they are merged here, duplicate events are joined and every source is kept.

## Summary

1. **Scope.** Three competition days (25-27 Sep), a packaging phase (28-29 Sep), a quiet gap with no recorded work (29 Sep 10:00 to 3 Oct 22:40), then finale preparation. Team Grenuke is Ameya (captain), Bakshi and Sachi.
2. **The climb.** Local holdout F0.5 rose from 0.9683 (baseline v0, 25 Sep 16:55) to 0.9913 (the 7B models, 27 Sep). Public LB F0.5 rose from 0.97608 (model v2) to 0.990879 (Composite B, the team's best, uploaded about 20:50 on 27 Sep). Candidates per S1 fell from about 34 (the raw blocking output) to 3.70 (the final candidate file).
3. **Turning point 1 (25 Sep 23:53).** Model v3 scored 0.97961 on the public LB against 0.98882 on the holdout. The gap pointed at France, the country with no labels (about 0.93 [E]). From then on France decided the ranking.
4. **Turning point 2 (26 Sep).** At 03:34 the root cause was found: in-place descriptor swaps ("op-B") that the model accepted in France but that are only 0.6% true. At 14:37 the first upload with the fixes (0.98781, rank 15) showed that the fixes carried over to the leaderboard.
5. **Turning point 3 (26 Sep about 23:35).** Self-training on France lifted the public LB by +0.000458 (rank 7). Larger cross-encoders (26 Sep) and a self-trained Qwen2.5-1.5B (27 Sep, +0.000281) followed.
6. **The last day (27 Sep).** Stacked decision rules, guarded self-training labels and Bakshi's Qwen2.5-7B re-check of confident pairs (Ameya: "the best France finding of the day") led to Composite B. Seven uploads were made; the last one (B7, about 23:40) tied B.
7. **Uploads.** Fifteen are listed, thirteen with a recorded public score (table below). The rank at upload was 15, 15, 8 and 7 on 26 Sep, then 7, 12 and 16 on 27 Sep. Between uploads the board moved: 17th at 21:12 on 26 Sep, 13th at 05:20 and 15th at 09:20 on 27 Sep, about 20th at 22:52. The private ranking is published as ranks only: Grenuke is 2nd of the Top 10.
8. **After the deadline.** The ZIP (Composite B, 146 files, 88 MB) was final on 29 Sep at 04:09. At 3 Oct 22:45 Ameya reported the Top-10 result: 32,000+ teams, Grenuke second on the list.
9. **Failures on the way.** A rented spot GPU was lost (26 Sep 21:40), the integration laptop froze (27 Sep 03:35), the tool's memory reaper killed background wrappers six times, and every ZIP that Bakshi's builder had produced before 27 Sep 09:28 was found broken.
10. **Open items.** Which upload the private ranking used (Composite B or B7), the order of the two uploads on 27 Sep morning, the 21:00 versus 23:59 window and the upload allowance are not settled. They are listed at the end of this page.

## How to read this page

- **Times** are IST. In a day table the date is the section's date. "~" means approximate. "n/r" means not recorded in the sources.
- **Numbers** are measured [M] (computed on labels, or scored by the leaderboard) unless tagged [E] estimated or [R] reported and not re-checked. "Holdout" is the local holdout; "LB" is the public leaderboard.
- **Bold** marks a turning point. The words Upload, Merge, Run, Failure and Plan change start the rows of that kind.
- **Who.** "Agent for Ameya" is the Claude Code agent working in Ameya's sessions. "Sub-agent" is an agent that it launched. Bakshi and Sachi appear with their own agents where the sources say so.
- **Sources** use the forms of [STANDARD §3](STANDARD.md). `[chat:ameya/19e315ba ...]` is Ameya's main session, `a2a1b62a` his parallel research session, `c0c64ad6` a side session, `agent-xxxxxxxx` a sub-agent.

### Terms used on this page

| term | meaning |
|---|---|
| S1, S2, S3 | The three record sources. S1 is the reference list (one record per business). S2 and S3 are two vendors' listings. The task: for each S1, list the S2/S3 records of the same business (0 to 11, mean 3.46). |
| F0.5 | The score: a precision-weighted F-measure per S1, averaged over S1 (macro). An S1 with no true match scores 1.0 only if the prediction is empty. |
| local holdout, dev | The holdout is a fixed 25% of labelled US/India S1 (549,699 S1), kept out of training. It has no France. "Dev" is a smaller sample (27,651 holdout S1) used on small machines. |
| public LB, private LB | The leaderboard during the challenge (a subset of the test set) and the final ranking (the rest of the test set). |
| blocking, candidates | The cheap first step that keeps likely pairs. Its output is `candidate_pairs.tsv`. Recall is the share of true pairs that survive. |
| stage 0, 1, 2, 3 | The model cascade. Stage 0 drops hopeless pairs. Stages 1 and 2 are XGBoost models; stage 2 adds features on rival S1 that compete for the same record, and cross-encoder scores. Stage 3 re-scores contested records. |
| pc, DP | `pc` is a pair's calibrated probability. The DP ("expected-F0.5 set selection") picks, per S1, the set of matches with the best expected F0.5, instead of one global threshold. |
| gate (G1-G13) | A test a component must pass, on a paired bootstrap of the holdout, before it enters the pipeline. |
| cross-encoder (CE), band | A text model that reads both records together. The band is the uncertain range of probabilities where it is used. e5, bge and Qwen are model families. |
| self-training, pseudo-labels | Training on the model's own confident decisions for France, which has no labels. |
| LOCO | Leave one country out: train on the US, test on India as a stand-in for the unseen France. |
| op-A, op-B | Two edits found in the French data. Op-A appends list words (mostly true copies). Op-B swaps a descriptor word in place (mostly decoys). |
| decoy | A record that looks like a match but is a different business. |
| rules v1-v3, `-dpc` | France rule sets built on op-A and op-B. `-dpc` marks the stacked final decision rules: the DP with a crowd shift, acronym and cap rules, plus copy and city polish for France. |
| `cal`, `fhs` | Label-free estimators of France's leaderboard gain. They proved biased on decoys (27 Sep 22:09). |
| 7B re-check | Bakshi's Qwen2.5-7B LoRA classifier re-reads confident predictions; those with logit below -6 are dropped as decoys. |
| composite, `mix...` | A per-country combination of several models' outputs. Composite B is the final submission. |
| rented boxes | Vast.ai GPU machines: H100 boxes 1-3 on 26 Sep, one H100 and two RTX 5090s (5090 #1, #2) on 27 Sep. |
| model names | v3-v7 are generations. Suffixes: `all` fit on all labelled data; `ce` cross-encoder logits; `n` mean of two large cross-encoders; `st` French self-training in stage 2; `s` adds a French self-trained e5-large; `q` adds Qwen2.5-1.5B; `g1w` Bakshi's 7B-in-stage-2 model. |
